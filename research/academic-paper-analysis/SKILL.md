## When to Use
Critically analyze academic papers: evaluate claims, methodology, evidence quality, replication potential.

## Reading Framework
1. **Abstract**: What's the claim?
2. **Introduction**: What gap does it fill?
3. **Method**: How did they test it? Is it reproducible?
4. **Results**: Do the data support the claims?
5. **Discussion**: What are the limitations?
6. **Related work**: Who do they cite? Who cites them?

## Critical Questions
- Is the sample size sufficient?
- Are there confounding variables?
- Was the study pre-registered?
- Can the results be replicated?
- Are the metrics appropriate?

## Pitfalls
- **Citation bias**: Highly cited ≠ correct
- **p-hacking**: Check if multiple comparisons were corrected
- **Survivorship bias**: Published results may not represent all attempts
- **Publication bias**: Negative results often go unpublished

## Verification
- Cross-reference with related papers
- Check reproducibility (code/data availability)
- Look for follow-up studies or corrections
