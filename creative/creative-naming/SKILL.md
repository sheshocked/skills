## When to Use
Generate names for products, companies, features, campaigns, apps, events, or internal projects. Use when launching something new, rebranding, or running a naming workshop.

## Core Concepts
- **Name types**: Descriptive (YouTube), Evocative (Nike), Invented (Kodak), Lexical (Facebook), Acronym (IBM), Founder (Dell), Metaphorical (Amazon).
- **Memorability factors**: Short (1-3 syllables), easy to spell, pronounceable, distinctive, emotionally resonant.
- **Domain + trademark check**: A great name is worthless if you can't own the .com and it's not trademarkable.
- **Cultural audit**: Check the name in all languages your audience speaks. "Muk" means something different in every culture.
- **Scalability**: A name that locks you into one product is a liability. Avoid overly literal names.

## Workflow
1. **Brief creation**: What does the product do? Who is it for? What feeling should the name evoke? What are the constraints?
2. **Brainstorming (divergent)**: Generate 200+ candidates. Use word association, Thesaurus, portmanteaus, foreign words, mythological references.
3. **Shortlisting (convergent)**: Apply filters — pronunciation, spelling, length, emotional resonance, relevance. Get to 20-30.
4. **Preliminary checks**: Domain availability (.com, .io, .co), social handle availability, basic trademark search (USPTO).
5. **Stakeholder review**: Present top 10 with rationale. Get feedback from team + 5-10 target users.
6. **Deep diligence**: Full trademark search (hire a lawyer), cultural sensitivity check, URL and social handle purchase.
7. **Final selection**: Make the decision. Don't committee-vote on naming — assign a decision-maker.

- Instagram = Instant + Telegram
- Pinterest = Pin + Interest
- FedEx = Federal + Express

Add common tech/product prefixes:
- -ify (simplify, clarify)
- -ly (friendlify, quickly)
- -io (dataio, buildio)

## Pitfalls
- **Falling in love with a name before checking availability**: Always check domain + trademark before announcing
- **Too clever**: If people can't spell it after hearing it once, it's too clever
- **Trendy suffixes**: -ify and -ly were hot in 2012. Trends date your brand instantly
- **Committee naming by committee**: Too many stakeholders = watered-down, safe, forgettable name
- **Ignoring international audiences**: A great English name can mean something terrible in another language
- **Narrow names**: "NYC Pizza Delivery" limits you when you expand to Brooklyn or add sushi

## Verification
- Tell 10 people the name verbally — can they spell it correctly without help?
- Search the name in quotes on Google — any existing products/brands with the same name?
- Check USPTO TESS database for trademark conflicts in relevant classes
- Verify .com domain is available (or acquirable for < $5,000)
- Test in dark mode: does the logo work on both light and dark backgrounds?
- Say it in a sentence: "I use [Name] for [task]." Does it flow naturally?
