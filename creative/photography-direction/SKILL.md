## When to Use
Direct photography shoots for products, portraits, editorial, events, or marketing. Use when planning a shoot, art-directing a photographer, building a shot list, or managing a visual content pipeline.

## Core Concepts
- **Composition rules**: Rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, negative space, symmetry. Learn them, then break them intentionally.
- **Lighting fundamentals**: Natural light (golden hour, blue hour), three-point lighting (key, fill, back), soft vs hard light.
- **Color theory in photography**: Complementary colors create pop. Analogous colors create harmony. Color grading sets mood.
- **Shutter speed/aperture/ISO triangle**: Aperture controls depth of field, shutter controls motion blur, ISO controls noise.
- **Art direction vs photography**: Art direction defines what the image should communicate. Photography executes the technical capture.
- **Brand visual consistency**: Style guide for photos: color palette, lighting style, composition patterns, subject treatment.

## Workflow
1. **Define the brief**: What is this photo for? Where will it be used? What should it communicate? Who is the audience?
2. **Mood board**: Collect 15-20 reference images that capture the desired aesthetic. Use Pinterest or Milanote.
3. **Shot list**: Create a detailed list of every shot needed. Include: subject, framing, lighting, background, props, mood.
4. **Location scouting**: Visit the location if possible. Note light conditions at different times. Identify problem areas.
5. **Pre-production**: Equipment check, model/talent booking, props procurement, wardrobe, hair/makeup.
6. **Shoot day**: Follow the shot list but leave room for creative moments. Review shots on set (chimping is okay here).
7. **Post-production**: Cull to best 10-20%. Edit in Lightroom/Capture One. Retouch selectively. Export for each use case.

```markdown
# Lighting Setup Diagrams
Three-Point Portrait:
  KEY LIGHT: 45° to subject, slightly above eye level, softbox
  FILL LIGHT: Opposite side, 50% intensity of key, umbrella
  BACK LIGHT: Behind subject, creates separation from background

Flat Product (e-commerce):
  TWO SOFTBOXES: 45° on each side, same height
  WHITE BACKDROP: Seamless paper or lightbox
  REFLECTORS: Fill shadows from below
```

## Pitfalls
- **No shot list**: Showing up without a plan wastes time and money. Even casual shoots benefit from a list
- **Ignoring the background**: A great subject with a distracting background ruins the image. Simplify backgrounds.
- **Wrong white balance**: Mixed lighting (tungsten + daylight) creates color casts. Set white balance manually.
- **Over-editing**: Heavy filters and excessive retouching look dated. Edit for consistency, not drama.
- **Not shooting RAW**: JPEG discards data. RAW preserves flexibility in post-production.
- **Ignoring aspect ratios**: Plan for where the image will be used. Instagram (1:1), stories (9:16), website (16:9 or 3:2).

## Verification
- Shot list completeness: Every deliverable in the brief has a corresponding shot
- Lighting consistency: All images in the series look like they belong together
- Brand alignment: Photos match the established visual style guide
- Technical quality: Sharp focus, correct exposure, no noise in shadows
- Usage check: Images work in all required formats (social, web, print) at their target sizes
