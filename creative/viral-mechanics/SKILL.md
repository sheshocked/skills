## When to Use
Design content, campaigns, or products with viral potential. Use when planning product launches, social campaigns, referral programs, or content strategies engineered for organic sharing.

## Core Concepts
- **K-factor**: Viral coefficient = (invitations sent per user) × (conversion rate). K > 1 = viral growth. Most products never reach K > 0.5.
- **Viral loops**: The mechanism by which users bring in new users. Must be frictionless and rewarding for both parties.
- **Share triggers**: Content gets shared when it triggers: awe, curiosity, anxiety, anger, joy, or social currency (makes the sharer look good).
- **Social currency**: People share things that make them look smart, funny, or in-the-know. Design for this.
- **Network effects**: The product becomes more valuable as more people use it. This is the strongest viral mechanic.
- **Dark patterns vs ethical virality**: Forced sharing is short-term gain, long-term trust destruction. Earned sharing is sustainable.

## Workflow
1. **Map the viral loop**: Step by step, how does a user encounter → use → share → bring a new user?
2. **Identify friction points**: Where do people drop off in the sharing process? Remove every unnecessary step.
3. **Design share triggers**: What specific emotion or social incentive will make someone share? Build it into the product/content.
4. **Incentivize sharing**: Referral rewards (both sides), exclusive content for sharers, visible milestones.
5. **Measure the funnel**: Track each step of the viral loop. Where is the biggest drop-off?
6. **Optimize iteratively**: Test different share CTAs, incentive structures, and content formats.
7. **Build network effects**: If possible, make the product better when more people use it.

## Key Patterns
```markdown
# Viral Loop Templates

## Referral Program Loop
User signs up → Gets unique referral link → Shares link → Friend clicks → Friend signs up → Both get reward
Optimization points:
- Sign-up friction (reduce to < 2 minutes)
- Share mechanism (one-click copy link, not manual)
- Reward value (must be meaningful — $5 credit > "exclusive badge")
- Timing of ask (after first "aha moment," not at sign-up)

## Content Virality Loop
Creator posts content → Algorithm shows to non-followers → Non-followers engage → Algorithm boosts reach → More non-followers see it → Repeat
Optimization points:
- Hook (first 3 seconds/first line)
- Engagement bait (questions, controversial takes, "agree or disagree?")
- Format (native to platform — don't fight the algorithm)

## Product Virality Loop
User creates something in product → Product adds branding/watermark → Non-user sees branded output → Clicks through → Signs up to create their own
Examples: Canva watermarks, Calendly links, Loom videos, Hotjar recordings

# Share Trigger Formula
"I shared this because it made me look [social currency trait]"
- Smart: "7 underrated Python libraries" (I know things others don't)
- Funny: Meme that only industry people understand (in-group signal)
- Helpful: Sharing a tool that solves a problem (being generous = social capital)
- Trendy: Early adopter of a new trend (I'm ahead of the curve)
```

## Pitfalls
- **Forcing virality**: Requiring shares to access core features creates resentment. Virality should be incentivized, not forced.
- **Ignoring the product**: Viral marketing for a bad product just creates more dissatisfied users faster
- **One-time viral hits**: A viral post doesn't create a sustainable business. Build repeatable viral mechanics, not one-off stunts.
- **K-factor obsession**: K > 1 is extremely rare. A K-factor of 0.3 combined with great retention still builds a massive business.
- **Copying viral tactics without understanding why they worked**: Context matters. What went viral for one brand may fail for another.

## Verification
- Map your viral loop end-to-end: every step is measurable
- K-factor calculation: invitations × conversion rate. Track weekly.
- Share rate: % of users who share. Benchmarks: 5%+ is good, 15%+ is excellent
- Referral conversion rate: % of referred users who sign up. Benchmarks: 10-20%
- Viral cycle time: How long from one user's action to the next user's action. Shorter = faster growth.
- Net Promoter Score: Would users recommend you? Score > 50 indicates strong viral potential
