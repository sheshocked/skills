## When to Use
Write high-converting sales copy, landing pages, email sequences, ad headlines, or any text where the goal is to move someone to action. Applies to B2B, B2C, SaaS, e-commerce, and personal branding.

## Core Concepts
- **AIDA**: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. The classic persuasion funnel.
- **PAS**: Problem → Agitation → Solution. Rub salt in the wound, then offer the bandage.
- **Features vs Benefits**: Features describe what it does. Benefits describe what it means for the reader.
- **Social Proof**: Testimonials, case studies, user counts, logos, media mentions.
- **Urgency/Scarcity**: Time limits, limited quantity, exclusive access. Must be genuine or it destroys trust.
- **One Reader Rule**: Write to a single person, not a crowd. "You" beats "customers" every time.
- **Curiosity Gap**: Tease information without giving it away. Headlines open loops; body copy closes them.

## Workflow
1. **Research**: Interview the customer. Find their exact words for pain points and desires. Mine reviews, forums, support tickets.
2. **Define the promise**: One sentence — what transformation does the product deliver?
3. **Choose framework**: PAS for cold audiences, AIDA for warm, Before-After-Bridge for nurture.
4. **Write the headline**: Spend 50% of your time here. Test 10+ variations.
5. **Draft body**: Lead with the problem, agitate it, present the solution, back with proof.
6. **Write CTA**: Specific action, clear benefit, low friction. "Start your free trial" beats "Submit."
7. **Edit ruthlessly**: Cut every word that doesn't serve the reader. Read aloud for rhythm.

## Key Patterns
```markdown
# Headline Templates That Work
- How [Audience] Can [Desired Outcome] Without [Pain Point]
- The [Adjective] Guide to [Topic] That [Benefit]
- [Number] [Things] That Will [Outcome] in [Timeframe]
- What Nobody Tells You About [Topic]
- Stop [Painful Activity]. Start [Desired Alternative].

# PAS Landing Page Structure
## [Problem Statement — mirrors their language]
Every day you [struggle with X]. You've tried [Y] and [Z], but nothing sticks.
## [Agitation — consequences of inaction]
Meanwhile, [competitors/people] are [achieving result], and you're still stuck...
## [Solution — your product as the bridge]
That's why we built [Product]. It [key differentiator] so you can [benefit].
## [Proof — social evidence]
"[testimonial from relatable person]" — [Name], [Title]
[logo bar of recognizable companies]
## [CTA — single clear action]
[Start Your Free Trial →]
```

## Pitfalls
- **Writing for yourself**: Your opinion doesn't matter. Only the reader's language and desires do.
- **Generic claims**: "Best-in-class solution" means nothing. Specific beats general: "Saves 4.2 hours/week."
- **Burying the CTA**: One clear action per page. Don't split attention with 5 buttons.
- **Ignoring mobile**: 60%+ of traffic is mobile. Short paragraphs, thumb-friendly CTAs.
- **No voice of customer data**: Guessing at pain points leads to copy that misses. Interview real users.
- **Overwriting**: Dense blocks of text get skipped. One idea per paragraph. Short sentences.

## Verification
- Run the "so what?" test on every line — if the reader wouldn't care, cut it
- A/B test headlines with Google Optimize or similar
- Read the copy aloud at speaking pace; if you stumble, rewrite
- Have someone unfamiliar with the product read it, then ask them what it sells and who it's for
- Check Flesch-Kincaid grade level: aim for 6-8 for consumer, 8-10 for B2B
- Verify all claims have evidence (stats, testimonials, case studies)
