## When to Use
Defining or refreshing a brand's visual identity: logo system, color palette, typography, imagery style, voice and tone. When establishing a new brand or consolidating fragmented brand expressions.

## Core Concepts
- **Brand pillars**: Values, personality, and positioning that inform visual decisions
- **Logo system**: Primary logo, secondary mark, favicon, clear space rules, minimum size
- **Visual language**: Photography style, illustration style, iconography, patterns
- **Brand voice**: Tone (formal/casual), personality traits, vocabulary rules, writing style
- **Co-branding rules**: How the brand appears alongside partners, sponsors, or sub-brands
- **Brand governance**: Approval process, asset library, usage guidelines

## Workflow
1. Define brand strategy: mission, values, audience, competitors, positioning
2. Develop mood boards with reference imagery aligned to brand personality
3. Design logo explorations (minimum 3 directions), refine final mark
4. Define color palette: primary, secondary, accent, neutral — with usage ratios
5. Select typography: primary typeface (display) + secondary (body), with usage rules
6. Create brand guidelines document (PDF or web-based)
7. Build digital asset library (Figma brand kit, shared drive)
8. Train teams on brand usage; set up review process for off-brand materials

## Key Patterns
- **Logo clear space rule**: Define minimum clear space as a unit based on logo geometry (e.g., "1x" = height of the logomark)
- **Color usage ratios**: 60% primary/neutral, 30% secondary, 10% accent — prevents visual overwhelm
- **Typography pairing rules**: Display font for headlines only, body font for everything else — never mix freely
- **Photography style guide**: "Candid, warm lighting, diverse subjects, real environments" — with do/don't examples
- **Tone of voice spectrum**: Map personality traits to writing decisions:
  ```
  Confident (not arrogant) | Friendly (not childish) | Expert (not jargon-heavy)
  ```
- **Logo minimum size**: Define smallest reproduction size for print (e.g., 24mm wide) and screen (e.g., 120px wide)

## Pitfalls
- Designing for personal taste instead of audience — brand must resonate with target users
- Too many fonts/colors — a strong brand needs restraint, not variety
- Logo that only works at one size — test at favicon (16px) through billboard scale
- No clear space rules — logos get cramped by surrounding elements
- Guidelines without examples — abstract rules confuse; show real applications
- Ignoring digital-specific needs — favicon, app icon, social media crops, dark mode logo

## Verification
- Show brand elements to 5 target-audience users — can they describe the brand personality?
- Apply brand to 5 real-world materials: website, business card, social post, presentation, email
- Check logo rendering at 16px (favicon) — still recognizable?
- Verify all brand assets are accessible: logo has text alternative, colors meet contrast ratios
- Test brand consistency: 10 screenshots from different parts of the product — consistent visual language?
