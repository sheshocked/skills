## When to Use
Craft the narrative foundation for a brand: origin story, mission narrative, brand voice guidelines, "about" pages, investor decks, or internal culture docs. Essential for startups, rebrands, and product launches.

## Core Concepts
- **Brand Archetype**: The 12 Jungian archetypes (Hero, Sage, Explorer, etc.) that shape brand personality and messaging tone.
- **Origin Story**: Not a timeline. It's the moment of insight — the "aha" that makes the brand exist. Must feel personal and relatable.
- **Brand Voice**: Consistent personality expressed through word choice, sentence structure, humor level, and formality.
- **Values Narrative**: Not a list of buzzwords. Stories that prove each value in action.
- **Audience as Hero**: The brand is the guide (Yoda), not the hero (Luke). The customer's journey is the story.

## Workflow
1. **Audit existing brand materials**: Gather all current copy, social posts, pitch decks, customer emails.
2. **Extract the core truth**: One sentence that captures why this brand exists beyond making money.
3. **Select archetype**: Pick 1 primary, 1 secondary. Example: Patagonia = Explorer + Caregiver.
4. **Write the origin story**: 150-300 words. Structure: Problem witnessed → Frustration → Insight → Mission → Current impact.
5. **Define voice guidelines**: 3-5 adjectives + opposites. "Confident, not arrogant. Playful, not childish."
6. **Create proof stories**: One story per value. Real events, not hypotheticals.
7. **Test with stakeholders**: Does it resonate? Can everyone on the team retell it?

## Key Patterns
```markdown
# Brand Voice Matrix
| Dimension | We Are | We Are Not |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Tone      | Warm, direct, witty | Cold, passive, sarcastic |
| Language  | Plain English, active voice | Jargon-heavy, passive voice |
| Humor     | Dry, self-deprecating | Forced, slapstick |
| Formality | Conversational-professional | Stuffy academic / overly casual |

# Origin Story Template
[Name] saw [problem] firsthand when [specific moment].
After [attempted solution that failed], [he/she/they] realized [insight].
That's why [Brand] exists: [mission in one sentence].
Today, [impact metric + transformation].

# Example (fictional SaaS):
Sarah spent 14 hours a week reconciling spreadsheets. After a costly error cost her company $40K, she built a tool that automates it. That's why DataSync exists: to eliminate manual data entry for finance teams. Today, 2,000+ companies save 12,000+ hours monthly.
```

## Pitfalls
- **Generic values**: "Innovation" and "integrity" mean nothing without proof stories
- **Copying competitors**: Your brand should feel distinct, not like a slightly different version of the market leader
- **Ignoring internal audience**: Employees need to live the brand story. If they can't retell it, it doesn't work
- **Changing voice every quarter**: Consistency builds recognition. Commit for at least 12 months
- **Founder ego**: The brand isn't about the founder's genius. It's about the customer's transformation

## Verification
- Ask 5 team members to independently describe the brand in one sentence — results should converge
- Compare your origin story to competitors: would swapping the name make it generic?
- Voice test: read 10 pieces of your copy together — do they sound like the same person?
- Customer test: show the brand story to 3 target customers and ask "does this feel like it was written for you?"
- Checklist: Origin story under 300 words? Voice has clear do/don't pairs? At least 3 proof stories per value?
