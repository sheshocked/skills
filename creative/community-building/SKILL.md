## When to Use
Build, grow, and sustain online or offline communities: Discord servers, Slack groups, forums, meetup groups, brand communities, or creator communities. Use for strategy, engagement, moderation, and monetization.

## Core Concepts
- **Community ≠ audience**: An audience listens to you. A community talks to each other. Design for member-to-member interaction.
- **Community lifecycle**: Seed → Grow → Engage → Sustain → Scale. Each stage requires different strategies.
- **Engagement pyramid**: 1% create content, 9% engage, 90% lurk. Design for all three. Don't force lurkers to create.
- **Community culture**: Set norms early. What's encouraged, what's tolerated, what's forbidden. Culture is set by what you allow, not what you prohibit.
- **Value exchange**: Members give time/attention/effort. They must receive value in return: knowledge, connection, access, status, or belonging.
- **Moderation**: The invisible hand of community health. Moderate early, moderate consistently, never make exceptions for friends.

## Workflow
1. **Define purpose**: Why does this community exist? What specific value do members get? "Learn X" or "Connect with people doing Y."
2. **Choose platform**: Discord (real-time, casual), Slack (professional, organized), Circle (course/community hybrid), Discourse (async, searchable), Meetup (IRL).
3. **Seed the community**: Invite 10-20 founding members personally. They set the culture. Choose people who will actively participate.
4. **Create content/rituals**: Weekly threads, daily prompts, AMAs, challenges, showcases. Consistent touchpoints build habits.
5. **Recognize members**: Highlight contributions. Badges, roles, shoutouts, featured member spotlights. Recognition drives behavior.
6. **Moderate actively**: Set rules in the first week. Enforce them immediately. Remove toxic members quickly — they destroy communities faster than you can build them.
7. **Scale carefully**: Only open the gates when the culture is established and self-sustaining.

## Key Patterns
```markdown
# Community Launch Checklist
- Platform chosen and configured
- Rules/guidelines posted (5-7 rules, clear and specific)
- Welcome message with onboarding steps
- 5-10 seed discussions started
- 10-20 founding members personally invited
- First event/meetup/AMA scheduled
- Moderation team assigned (at least 2 moderators)
- Welcome DM sent to every new member

# Engagement Rituals (Weekly)
Monday: "What are you working on this week?"
Wednesday: "Show & Tell — share what you built/learned"
Friday: "Wins thread — celebrate progress, no matter how small"
Monthly: AMA with an expert or notable community member
Quarterly: Community retrospective — what's working, what's not

# Moderation Framework
DO:
- Enforce rules consistently, including for friends and power users
- Respond to reports within 24 hours
- Explain moderation decisions publicly (builds trust)
- Reward positive behavior more than punishing negative

DON'T:
- Let slide "just this once" — precedent destroys rules
- Moderate in anger — always pause before removing/banning
- Play favorites — perceived bias kills community trust
- Ignore toxic behavior — one bad actor can destroy a community

# Community Health Metrics
| Metric | Good | Great | Action if below |
|--------|------|-------|----------------|
| Daily active users / total members | 10-20% | 20-30% | More engagement rituals |
| Messages per day | 50+ | 200+ | Seed more discussions |
| New members per week | 5-10% growth | 10-20% growth | Improve invite/referral |
| Member-to-member replies | 60%+ of messages | 80%+ | You're talking too much — step back |
| Response time to questions | < 2 hours | < 30 min | Recruit more active members |
```

## Pitfalls
- **Founder as bottleneck**: If you stop posting, the community dies. Design for self-sustaining interaction.
- **Growing too fast**: Rapid growth dilutes culture. Gate access until culture is established.
- **Ignoring lurkers**: 90% of members lurk. That's fine. Design value for them too (curated content, reading).
- **No clear purpose**: "A community for everyone" = a community for no one. Be specific.
- **Toxic members tolerated too long**: One toxic person drives away 10 good members. Act decisively.
- **Platform lock-in**: Own your member list (email). Platforms can change, shut down, or degrade.

## Verification
- Member-to-member conversations happening without your involvement
- Community rules clearly posted and consistently enforced
- At least 3-5 "power users" who contribute regularly without being asked
- New members receive a welcome within 24 hours of joining
- Health metrics tracked weekly: DAU, messages, response time, new member growth
- Zero unresolved moderation reports older than 48 hours
